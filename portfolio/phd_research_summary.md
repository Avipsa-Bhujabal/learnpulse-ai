# PhD research summary

LearnPulse studies whether within-person behavioral change transfers across educational contexts when predicting a narrowly defined outcome: no recorded VLE activity in a subsequent 14-day course window. The motivation is that absolute engagement levels can obscure individual routines, while personal baselines may reveal meaningful deviations.

The research combines temporally ordered feature construction, earlier-window-only baselines, student-grouped validation and complete course holdouts. It examines observation timing, common-cohort sensitivity, coefficient stability, nested calibration, subgroup error patterns and warning trajectories. A retrospective queue simulation asks how limited human-review capacity alters alert access, while making no intervention-effect claim.

The findings support further investigation of personal-change signals but also show heterogeneity, uncertainty, score instability across separately fitted day models and operational trade-offs. Future research should involve prospective shadow evaluation, participatory outcome design, institutional workflow observation, stronger missingness analysis and predeclared human-support protocols. Predictions must remain subject to human review and cannot establish motivation or causal need.
